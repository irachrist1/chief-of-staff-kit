# Setup check

Send this to an agent once it says it's set up, and again whenever a new model or version comes out. It makes the agent prove what works instead of telling you what it thinks it can do.

> Setup check. Go through your brief line by line: every duty, routine, rule and example in it. Test each one now, for real, instead of telling me what you think you can do. Keep the tests safe: read, search, open, draft, and put a test block on my own calendar then remove it. You may also do the things the brief already lets you do without my yes, like posting in the team room or saving your report card in your own folder. Don't send to anyone else, pay, submit or delete anything.
>
> Then give me one table, in the order of the brief:
>
> | What the brief asks | Status | How you tested it | What's missing, and what you need from me |
>
> Status is one of: **working** (you tested it just now and it worked), **ready** (everything it needs is in place, and it only waits for a real event, like a meeting or a big decision), **partly** (some of it works, or it's set up but you haven't seen it run yet), **not yet** (you could do it once I connect or give you something), or **can't here** (this platform doesn't allow it). If a duty can be done right now, do it instead of marking it ready.
>
> Before the table, list:
> - Every tool and account you can reach, and every one you can't.
> - Every routine you've set, with when it next runs. Mark any that don't come from the brief, and say whether to keep them.
> - Whether the brief is saved in your memory or instructions, and which version.
> - Whether you've posted in the team room today, if the brief has one.
>
> After the table, give me:
> - The count for each status.
> - The three things I could give you today that would turn the most "not yet" into "working".
> - Anything in the brief you think is wrong, unclear or impossible.
>
> Don't round up. "Partly" is fine. A "working" that turns out to be wrong is not.
