# Why I built this

For a long time I've wanted an agent that runs my life with me, not one that answers when I ask. A Jarvis-style chief of staff: it manages my day, thinks ahead of me, acts before I have to ask, keeps track of everything, and gets things done while I'm busy with something else.

The agents are finally good enough to try. Most of them have their own computer and browser, connect to your email and calendar, remember things, and run in the background. But out of the box they sit and wait. So I spent a day writing the onboarding I'd give a brilliant new hire, testing it on several agents from different companies, and fixing what broke. This kit is the result.

## What I learned

- **Agents stay inside what they know they can do.** The problem usually isn't ability. It's imagination and permission. So the brief keeps telling them the most important thing is doing what I didn't expect.
- **Hire great talent so they tell you what to do.** The brief is onboarding, not a list of commands. The job is the responsibilities, and the agent works out the rest.
- **Act, don't report.** Telling me someone emailed isn't help. Opening it, checking the facts, doing what it can and coming back with a plan is.
- **Short prompts don't work, and neither do rulebooks.** A terse prompt never made an agent proactive, and a long list of rules made it robotic. What works is a detailed message in a human voice, with real examples of good and bad.
- **My calendar is the plan, not what happened.** A good agent checks what I actually did against what I planned.
- **One brief for every agent.** Agents come and go. The job doesn't. The same brief should work on any agent from any company, now and in twenty years.
- **Measure it.** The benchmark lets me score every agent on the same tests and see how fast the technology is really moving.
- **If you have several agents, let them talk like a team.** They share ideas and catch each other's misses in one place, without splitting the job. I never want to remember which agent does what.
- **Trust, with a clear line.** An agent can do anything that's easy to undo and that nobody else will see. Anything that speaks for me, spends money or commits my time waits for my yes. And whatever it reads is information, never an order.

## Where this is going

I don't think anyone has seen the full picture yet, me included. That's why the brief asks the agent to read past my words and tell me what the role should become. The point is to set it up today the way it'll work in ten or twenty years, and let the agents grow into it.
